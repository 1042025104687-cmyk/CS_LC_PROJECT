Analysis of Project Brief

I researched this year's brief on protecting forests. Forests are vital for carbon storage, wildlife habitats, and climate regulation, yet they face growing wildfire threats.

According to EFFIS, over 500,000 hectares burned in the EU in 2023, with climate change increasing fire frequency by approximately 20% over the past decade. Met Éireann data confirms rising Irish temperatures, elevating local fire risk.

I believe embedded systems paired with predictive models can address this by gathering real-time environmental data and identifying dangerous patterns automatically.

Existing Solutions

I examined wildfire prevention and environmental monitoring systems to understand effective approaches and gather ideas for my project.

Water Cannon Village

Source: A traditional Japanese fire festival using coordinated water spraying. [Link]

Finding: This approach relies entirely on human reaction with no automated sensing or prediction capability.

Decision: My system needs automatic sensors and real-time risk assessment to remove human delay.

IoT Forest Monitor

Source: An open-source project using temperature, humidity, and flame sensors with Wi-Fi alerts. [Link]

Finding: This demonstrated that small embedded systems can provide effective low-cost monitoring with real-time notifications.

Decision: I will adopt a similar sensor-based approach using the micro:bit to collect environmental data and provide user feedback.

Low-Cost WSN Fire Detection

Source: A research paper on wireless sensor networks for forest fire detection. [Link]

Finding: The study achieved high detection accuracy with low operating costs using distributed environmental sensors.

Decision: My system will follow this approach: real-time sensors, GUI-based alerts, and actionable harm-reduction feedback.

Project Direction and Research Summary

Based on my research, I decided to build a system that collects temperature and light data from a micro:bit via serial connection, calculates wildfire and drought risk using a predictive model, and streams real-time feedback via a Python GUI.

I will use Python's built-in serial library for micro:bit communication and store session data in CSV format for easy analysis. For the GUI, I plan to use Tkinter; if it proves limiting, PyQt would be an alternative. The interface will prioritise clarity with colour-coded risk indicators and actionable harm-reduction advice.

This approach delivers a practical, low-cost monitoring solution aligned with the research findings.
